# Visual Direction

## Purpose

This document defines the visual direction for the Baghlaf Steel website.

It establishes the overall:

- visual character;
- aesthetic territory;
- emotional impression;
- art direction;
- composition;
- imagery;
- typography direction;
- color direction;
- motion philosophy;
- visual boundaries;
- relationship between industrial credibility and international B2B professionalism.

This document provides the creative direction for Figma and frontend implementation.

It does not define final design tokens, component specifications, or implementation details.

Those are maintained in:

```text
design-system.md
typography.md
color-system.md
responsive-rules.md
rtl-rules.md
component-guidelines.md

1. Visual Direction
Primary Direction

Industrial Editorial

The Baghlaf Steel website should occupy a visual position between:

Traditional Industrial
        ↕
Modern International B2B
        ↕
Editorial / Premium

The result should feel:

Industrial
+
Precise
+
Mature
+
Structured
+
Modern
+
Credible
+
International
+
Human

The design should communicate confidence through composition, information quality, typography, imagery, and consistency.

It should not rely on visual effects to create credibility.

2. Desired First Impression

When an international buyer first encounters the website, the intended impression is:

"This is a serious company."
        ↓
"They clearly understand their role."
        ↓
"The information feels organized and precise."
        ↓
"I can understand what they supply."
        ↓
"I can verify who they are."
        ↓
"I know how to engage with them."

The visual design should support this progression.

3. Brand Character

The visual identity should communicate:

Precision

The interface should feel measured and intentional.

Use:

strong alignment;
disciplined spacing;
structured grids;
clear typography;
consistent components.

Avoid visual randomness.

Industrial Strength

The design should feel connected to:

steel;
infrastructure;
logistics;
materials;
engineering;
large-scale commercial activity.

This should be achieved through visual language rather than clichés.

Professional Maturity

The website should feel established and commercially serious.

Avoid visual trends that make the company appear:

experimental;
playful;
consumer-focused;
startup-generic.
International Orientation

The website should feel suitable for buyers from different countries and professional backgrounds.

This means:

clear information;
restrained language;
strong hierarchy;
internationally recognizable technical notation;
professional imagery;
bilingual readiness.
Human Credibility

The website should not feel like an anonymous industrial catalog.

People, processes, evidence, and real context should be visible where appropriate.

Human presence should feel authentic rather than staged.

4. Emotional Tone

The intended emotional tone is:

Confident
Calm
Precise
Serious
Trustworthy
Capable
Modern
Professional

Avoid creating emotions associated with:

Hype
Urgency
Luxury Excess
Artificial Futurism
Playfulness
Aggressive Salesmanship
5. Industrial Editorial

Industrial Editorial is the central visual concept.

It combines:

Industrial Context
+
Editorial Composition
+
Technical Information
+
Strong Typography
+
Structured Layout

The website should feel more like a carefully designed international industrial publication than a generic corporate template.

6. Editorial Composition

Use composition intentionally.

Potential techniques include:

Large Headlines
Asymmetric Grids
Strong Margins
Editorial Columns
Large Photography
Technical Data Blocks
Controlled Negative Space
Unexpected but Structured Alignment

Asymmetry should remain controlled.

The page should feel intentional rather than chaotic.

7. Grid Philosophy

The grid should provide:

Order
Rhythm
Alignment
Hierarchy
Consistency

The grid should be strong enough to establish discipline while flexible enough to support editorial compositions.

Do not force every section into identical card rows.

8. Whitespace

Whitespace is an important part of the visual identity.

Use whitespace to create:

Hierarchy
Calm
Premium Perception
Readability
Separation
Focus

Premium perception should come from:

Typography
Spacing
Photography
Composition
Consistency

not from gold effects, excessive gradients, or decorative luxury styling.

9. Typography Direction

Typography should be one of the strongest visual elements.

The direction favors:

Strong Editorial Headlines
Clear Body Typography
Controlled Weight Contrast
Technical Detail
Generous Rhythm

Typography must remain highly readable.

The exact typefaces and token values are defined separately in:

typography.md
10. Headline Character

Headlines should feel:

Strong
Direct
Confident
Editorial

Avoid excessively promotional headline patterns.

Prefer clarity over cleverness.

The headline should help communicate the actual business rather than simply create visual drama.

11. Body Copy

Body copy should support the visual system through:

comfortable line length;
clear hierarchy;
restrained density;
predictable rhythm.

The website should never rely on huge text blocks to communicate basic business information.

12. Color Direction

The palette should feel compatible with:

Steel
Architecture
Infrastructure
Industrial Materials
Professional B2B Environments

The final brand palette is not yet established in this document.

Exact colors belong in:

color-system.md

The visual direction should favor a restrained palette with carefully controlled accent usage.

13. Contrast

Contrast should establish hierarchy.

Potential contrast relationships include:

Light Surface
+
Dark Editorial Surface

Muted Neutral
+
Strong Accent

Small Metadata
+
Large Headline

Contrast should support information rather than become decoration.

14. Dark Editorial Surfaces

Dark sections may be used strategically.

They can provide:

visual rhythm;
contrast;
industrial character;
emphasis.

They should remain part of the broader visual system rather than becoming a separate dark theme.

15. Gradients

Gradients are not a primary part of the visual direction.

Avoid:

Large Decorative Gradients
Rainbow Gradients
Neon Gradients
Futuristic Glow Effects
SaaS-Style Gradient Backgrounds

A restrained gradient may be acceptable only when it serves a clear compositional or functional purpose.

16. Glassmorphism

Glassmorphism is not a core Baghlaf Steel visual direction.

Avoid making the interface depend on:

translucent glass cards;
blurred floating panels;
excessive transparency;
layered glass effects.

A subtle translucent treatment may be used as a functional detail where justified, but it should never define the visual identity.

17. Rounded Shapes

Corner radius should be restrained.

Avoid:

Everything Rounded
Excessive Pill Buttons
Bubble-Like Cards
Overly Soft Interfaces

Industrial Editorial should retain some structural sharpness.

The exact radius system belongs in:

design-system.md
18. Cards

Cards should be used selectively.

They are appropriate when information genuinely benefits from grouping.

Do not turn every section into:

Card
Card
Card
Card

Editorial layouts, technical tables, open content blocks, and structured lists should remain available.

19. Photography Direction

Photography should feel:

Authentic
Industrial
Cinematic Where Appropriate
Professional
Documentary-Inspired
Contextual

Potential subject matter:

Steel
Metal Surfaces
Industrial Environments
Manufacturing Context
Ports
Logistics
Infrastructure
Warehouses
Materials
Engineering
People at Work
Saudi Industrial Context

The exact image library should be selected and approved separately.

20. Industrial Photography

Industrial imagery should communicate:

Scale
Precision
Materiality
Process
Professionalism

Avoid images that look like generic stock photography whenever a more authentic option exists.

21. Facility Imagery

Facility photographs require special caution.

A photograph of a steel mill or factory may visually imply:

"This is Baghlaf Steel's facility."

That implication must not be made unless ownership, operation, or approved relationship is established.

Where necessary, imagery should be contextualized to make the relationship clear.

22. People

People may be included to humanize the business.

Use realistic professional contexts.

Avoid:

Overly Posed Corporate Teams
Generic Stock Handshakes
Fake Office Scenes
Unrelated Business Portraits

The human element should support credibility rather than look staged.

23. Saudi Identity

Saudi identity should be communicated through authenticity rather than visual clichés.

Prefer:

Saudi Industrial Context
Architecture
Infrastructure
Local Professional Environment
Regional Geography
Authentic Business Context

Avoid overusing:

Flags
Green Everywhere
Decorative Arabic Motifs
Generic Desert Imagery
National Symbols as Decoration

Saudi identity should feel integrated, not pasted onto an international industrial design.

24. International B2B Character

The visual language should feel equally appropriate for:

Saudi Arabia
GCC
Middle East
Asia
Europe
Africa
Other International Markets

without attempting to imitate a specific foreign corporate style.

The design should communicate global professionalism through clarity and quality.

25. Technical Visual Language

Technical information can become part of the visual identity.

Useful elements may include:

Specifications
Measurements
Standards
Grades
Tables
Reference Numbers
Structured Data
Diagrams
Documentation

Technical information should feel organized and intentional rather than visually hidden.

26. Materiality

Steel and industrial materials may inspire visual language through:

Texture
Surface
Structure
Weight
Precision
Geometry

Do not overuse literal metal textures.

The design should suggest materiality without becoming a themed "steel texture" website.

27. Geometry

Geometry may be inspired by:

steel sections;
architectural forms;
grids;
structural lines;
industrial geometry.

Use geometric elements selectively.

They should support composition rather than become decorative clutter.

28. Lines and Dividers

Lines may reinforce the technical/editorial character.

Use them to create:

Grouping
Alignment
Hierarchy
Technical Structure

Avoid decorative line patterns with no informational purpose.

29. Visual Density

The system should support multiple information densities.

Marketing Content

More whitespace and stronger editorial emphasis.

Product Content

Higher information density.

Technical Data

Structured, compact information.

Requirement Flow

Clear, focused interaction.

Different densities should still feel like one system.

30. Trust Through Design

Trust should come from:

Consistency
Clarity
Evidence
Professional Typography
Accurate Information
Official Identity
Predictable Interaction

Avoid fake trust signals such as:

Fake Security Badges
Fake Ratings
Fake Verification Icons
Random Certification Logos
Artificial Counters
31. Verification Visual Language

The Verification experience should feel:

Official
Clear
Factual
Structured
Calm

It should not resemble:

Banking Security Dashboard
Cybersecurity Product UI
Alarm / Warning System

unless a specific design need requires those metaphors.

The goal is confidence through clarity.

32. Commercial Action Visual Language

The primary commercial action should be visually clear.

Current strategic direction:

Submit Requirement

The button should feel important without resembling:

Buy Now
Checkout
Consumer Purchase

unless the business model later explicitly supports those actions.

33. CTA Hierarchy

The design should support:

Primary
→
Secondary
→
Tertiary

The visual hierarchy should correspond to actual user intent.

Primary commercial and trust actions should not compete equally with every secondary link.

34. Motion Direction

Motion should feel:

Calm
Controlled
Purposeful
Fast Enough
Professional

Use motion to communicate:

state changes;
interaction;
hierarchy;
navigation;
feedback.

Do not use motion simply because the interface feels "too static."

35. Motion Avoidances

Avoid:

Excessive Parallax
Continuous Floating
Aggressive Scroll Effects
Heavy Particle Effects
Overly Long Transitions
Decorative Motion Everywhere

Motion should not distract buyers from technical and commercial information.

36. Hover Effects

Hover interactions may provide subtle feedback.

Examples:

Button State
Card Interaction
Link State
Image Treatment

Do not make important information available only on hover.

37. Accessibility and Motion

Motion should respect reduced-motion preferences.

The visual experience should remain fully understandable when motion is reduced or disabled.

38. Responsive Visual Direction

The Industrial Editorial character must survive across:

Desktop
Tablet
Mobile

Desktop may emphasize:

Large Scale
Wide Composition
Editorial Asymmetry

Mobile should preserve the same character through:

Strong Typography
Clear Rhythm
Focused Imagery
Structured Information

Do not allow mobile to become a generic stack of cards.

39. RTL Visual Direction

The Industrial Editorial character should remain consistent in:

LTR
RTL

Arabic should feel intentionally designed rather than mechanically mirrored.

Follow:

rtl-rules.md

for detailed behavior.

40. Visual Continuity Across Languages

English and Arabic should share:

Visual Identity
Hierarchy
Brand Character
Component Language
Interaction Patterns

Differences should result from language and direction, not from independent visual systems.

41. Design References

External references may be used to inspire:

composition;
typography;
grid systems;
industrial photography;
information presentation;
interaction patterns.

Potential sources include:

Dribbble
Behance
Awwwards
Pinterest
Industrial Websites
Architecture / Editorial References

References are inspiration only.

They do not override:

Baghlaf Brand Direction
Approved Figma
Business Truth
Project Constitution
Design System
42. Reference Selection Criteria

A visual reference should be evaluated based on:

Does it fit Industrial Editorial?
Does it support B2B credibility?
Does it improve information clarity?
Can it work responsively?
Can it work in Arabic / RTL?
Can it be implemented realistically?
Does it avoid generic AI/SaaS patterns?
43. Avoiding AI-Looking Design

The website should avoid visual patterns that commonly make AI-generated interfaces feel generic.

Avoid excessive:

Gradient Blobs
Glass Cards
Random Floating Shapes
3D Illustrations
Huge Decorative Icons
Excessive Rounded Cards
Generic SaaS Dashboard Patterns
Repeated "Feature Card" Sections
Artificially Perfect Symmetry

AI should be used to accelerate implementation, not to define a generic AI aesthetic.

44. Brand Assets

The final visual system should use approved:

Logo
Logo Variants
Colors
Fonts
Photography
Icons
Illustrations
Patterns

Do not invent replacement brand assets unless explicitly requested and approved.

45. Logo Usage

The logo should have defined:

clear space;
minimum size;
approved variants;
background usage;
light/dark usage.

Detailed specifications should come from the official brand system or approved Figma.

46. Color + Logo Consistency

Logo colors must remain consistent with the approved brand system.

Do not recolor the logo simply to make it match an individual page.

47. Imagery Consistency

Photography should maintain a coherent visual character across the website.

Avoid combining:

Ultra-Realistic Industrial Photography
+
Cartoon Illustrations
+
3D Rendered Objects
+
Generic Stock Office Photos

without a deliberate art-direction reason.

48. Illustration Direction

Illustration is not a primary visual language for the site.

If illustrations are used, they should be:

Technical
Minimal
Geometric
Editorial
Purposeful

Avoid playful SaaS illustrations.

49. Decorative Graphics

Decorative elements should have a clear relationship to the Industrial Editorial system.

Potential elements:

Structural Lines
Geometry
Technical Markers
Grid References
Material-Inspired Shapes

Use sparingly.

50. Premium Perception

The website should feel premium through:

Craftsmanship
Typography
Spacing
Photography
Composition
Information Quality
Consistency

not through:

Gold
Chrome
Gloss
Luxury Gradients
Excessive Animation
51. Visual Consistency

Every major page should feel unmistakably related to the same system.

Consistency should exist across:

Homepage
Products
Product Detail
Supply Network
How We Work
Why Baghlaf
About
Verification
Contact
Resources
Requirement Flow

The design should evolve by content and context, not by arbitrary style changes.

52. Page-Level Flexibility

A consistent design system does not require identical pages.

Different pages may have distinct visual emphasis.

For example:

Homepage
→ Editorial / Brand

Product Detail
→ Technical / Informational

Verification
→ Factual / Trust

Requirement
→ Focused / Functional

All should remain part of the same visual language.

53. Visual Hierarchy

Every page should establish:

Primary
Secondary
Supporting

through:

scale;
spacing;
contrast;
typography;
placement.

Do not make every element equally prominent.

54. Visual Rhythm

Pages should create rhythm through:

Large Section
→
Compact Information
→
Large Section
→
Technical Detail
→
CTA

The exact rhythm depends on page purpose.

Avoid monotonous repetition.

55. Visual Storytelling

Visual storytelling should support the buyer's understanding.

A useful sequence may be:

Context
→
Capability
→
Evidence
→
Process
→
Trust
→
Action

Visuals should reinforce the information hierarchy.

56. Trust and Authenticity

Visual authenticity is important because the website exists partly to address uncertainty around Baghlaf's digital identity.

The design should therefore prioritize:

Real Information
Real Evidence
Real Identity
Real Context

over visual spectacle.

57. Content-Led Design

Content should influence design.

Do not force information into a predetermined visual template when doing so harms clarity.

Especially for:

Technical Specifications
Credentials
Verification
Requirement Forms
Supply Relationships

the layout should adapt to the information.

58. Design Exceptions

A visual exception is acceptable when it has a strong reason.

Examples:

Special Campaign
Important Business Announcement
Technical Visualization
Interactive Map
Major Resource

The exception should remain consistent with the broader brand.

59. Design Decision Protocol

When a design question arises:

1. Check approved Figma.
2. Check design-system rules.
3. Check this visual direction.
4. Check UX requirements.
5. Check business constraints.
6. Choose the simplest solution consistent with all of the above.

Do not introduce a new visual language for a local problem.

60. AI Design Rules

AI agents working on design should:

follow Industrial Editorial;
inspect existing Figma;
reuse approved components;
use approved tokens;
preserve hierarchy;
avoid generic SaaS patterns;
avoid excessive gradients;
avoid glassmorphism as a core language;
avoid decorative visual noise;
preserve accessibility;
preserve responsive behavior;
preserve RTL behavior;
never invent brand assets;
never use imagery that implies unsupported company ownership.

AI may suggest alternatives, but approved design direction takes precedence.

61. Visual QA

Visual validation should compare implementation against approved design.

Review:

Composition
Typography
Color
Spacing
Imagery
Components
Motion
Responsive Behavior
RTL

Significant deviations should be identified and corrected.

62. Design Quality Bar

A successful page should feel:

Intentional
Cohesive
Precise
Professional
Distinctive

It should not feel:

Template-Based
AI-Generated
Overdesigned
Generic SaaS
Visually Noisy

Final Rule

Baghlaf Steel should look credible because the design is disciplined, the information is precise, and the experience is authentic.

The visual goal is not to make the website look expensive or futuristic.

The goal is to make it feel:

Strong
+
Precise
+
Mature
+
Industrial
+
Editorial
+
International
+
Trustworthy

while remaining unmistakably coherent across every page, language, and viewport.
