# Baghlaf Steel — Buyer Journey & Conversion

> Strategic framework for how international B2B buyers should discover, understand, evaluate, verify, and engage with Baghlaf Steel through the website.

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the strategic buyer journey and conversion model for the Baghlaf Steel website.

It establishes:

- How buyers may enter the website
- What questions they need answered
- What information they need at each stage
- How trust affects the journey
- How product discovery connects to commercial engagement
- How the requirement submission experience should work
- How different buyer-intent levels should be supported
- How conversion should be measured

This document defines strategic UX and conversion direction.

It does not replace the detailed UX specifications in:

`docs/01-strategy/12-ux-wireframes-and-page-experience.md`

nor the actual commercial workflow and requirement fields that must be confirmed by Baghlaf management.

4. Buyer Journey Philosophy

The buyer journey should progressively reduce uncertainty.

Unknown
   ↓
Understanding
   ↓
Evaluation
   ↓
Verification
   ↓
Confidence
   ↓
Commercial Engagement

Every major page should help answer at least one important buyer question.

The website should not force buyers through unnecessary steps simply to increase page views.

5. Entry Points

International buyers may enter the website through:

Homepage
Product page
Product search
Search engine result
Shared product URL
Verification page
Contact page
Direct requirement link
Company/about content
External referral

The architecture must support entry into important pages without assuming that the visitor has already seen the homepage.

6. Entry-Point Principle

Every important landing page should provide enough context to answer:

Where am I?
What is Baghlaf Steel?
Why is this information relevant?
What can I do next?

A product page shared directly with a procurement colleague should still make sense independently.

7. Buyer Intent Model

The website should recognize three broad levels of intent.

7.1 Low Intent

The visitor is primarily learning.

Typical questions:

Who is Baghlaf?
What does the company do?
Where is it based?
How does it operate?

Typical actions:

About
How We Work
Products
Supply Network
Verification
7.2 Medium Intent

The visitor is evaluating Baghlaf's potential suitability.

Typical questions:

Can Baghlaf potentially supply what I need?
What products are available through the company?
How does sourcing work?
What is the relationship with supply sources?
Is the company legitimate?

Typical actions:

Product Detail
Supply Network
Technical Information
Verification
Contact
7.3 High Intent

The visitor is ready to start a commercial conversation.

Typical questions:

How do I submit my requirement?
What information should I provide?
Can Baghlaf evaluate my request?
What happens after submission?

Typical actions:

Submit Requirement
Request This Product
Contact

   ↓
MEDIUM INTENT
Evaluate + Verify
   ↓
HIGH INTENT

However, visitors must be able to skip directly to the action appropriate to their current intent.

33. Error Recovery

A conversion flow should help the buyer recover from problems.

Examples:

Validation Error
→ Show exact problem

Upload Error
→ Explain acceptable file requirements

Network Error
→ Preserve entered information where possible

Submission Error
→ Tell user whether submission succeeded

Avoid vague messages such as:

"Something went wrong."

57. Final Strategic Principle

The Baghlaf Steel website should not function as a forced linear marketing funnel. It should operate as a buyer-centered environment that allows international B2B visitors to enter at different levels of intent, understand the company's actual role, evaluate relevant supply capability, independently verify the official identity, and move into legitimate commercial engagement when ready. The primary commercial conversion is submission of a structured steel requirement, while verification remains an independent and equally important trust action. Conversion should be optimized through clarity, evidence, reduced friction, and professional UX—not through unsupported claims or manipulative tactics.
