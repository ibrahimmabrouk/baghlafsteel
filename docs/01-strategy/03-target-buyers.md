# Baghlaf Steel — Target Buyers

> Strategic definition of the audiences the Baghlaf Steel website is intended to serve, the problems they are trying to solve, and the information and actions they need from the website.

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the strategic target-buyer model for the Baghlaf Steel website.

It establishes:

- Who the website is primarily designed for
- What these visitors are trying to accomplish
- What information they need
- What concerns may prevent engagement
- How the website should support their decision-making
- How buyer needs should influence UX, content, navigation, and conversion

This document defines strategic audience direction.

It does not replace actual customer records, approved market data, or management-confirmed customer segmentation.

Confirmed commercial information should be stored in:

`docs/02-business/`

---

# 2. Primary Audience

The primary website audience is:

# International B2B Steel Buyers

These are business users seeking steel products, steel supply, or a suitable commercial supply route through Baghlaf Steel.

The website should therefore be designed primarily for professional buyers rather than for:

- General consumers
- Casual visitors
- Lifestyle audiences
- Generic business traffic

---

# 3. Primary Buyer Objective

The central buyer objective is:

> **Determine whether Baghlaf Steel can provide a credible and suitable path to fulfill a steel requirement.**

The buyer may need to determine:

```text
Who is Baghlaf?
      ↓
What does Baghlaf do?
      ↓
What can Baghlaf potentially supply?
      ↓
Where can the material be sourced?
      ↓
How does the commercial process work?
      ↓
Can I verify Baghlaf?
      ↓
How do I submit my requirement?

The website should support this progression without forcing every visitor through the same path.

4. Buyer Types

The current strategic model recognizes several broad buyer types.

These are strategic audience categories rather than confirmed customer segments.

4.1 Procurement Professionals
Description

Professionals responsible for identifying, evaluating, purchasing, or sourcing steel for an organization.

Primary Needs

They may need:

Clear product information
Technical specifications
Supply capability
Commercial contact
Requirement submission
Verification of the supplier/trading company
Website Priority

High

The website should allow procurement users to move quickly from:

Product
   ↓
Relevant information
   ↓
Requirement
4.2 Steel / Commodity Buyers
Description

Professionals who actively participate in steel or commodity purchasing and may understand market terminology and commercial processes.

Primary Needs

They may prioritize:

Product capability
Specifications
Quantity
Supply source
Commercial process
Transaction requirements
Website Priority

High

The website should provide sufficiently precise information without forcing experienced buyers through overly simplified marketing content.

4.3 Industrial Buyers
Description

Organizations purchasing steel for industrial, infrastructure, construction, fabrication, manufacturing, or other business requirements.

Primary Needs

Depending on the requirement, they may need:

Product fit
Technical specifications
Standards
Quantity
Destination
Delivery requirements
Documentation
Commercial contact
Website Priority

High

Exact industrial segments should be confirmed through management/customer data before being treated as formal target-market categories.

4.4 Large-Volume Buyers
Description

Buyers with substantial steel requirements who may require structured commercial coordination.

Primary Needs

They may prioritize:

Quantity
Product specifications
Supply capability
Sourcing route
Commercial terms
Logistics
Documentation
Direct communication
Website Priority

High

The website should support serious, detailed requirements without assuming a specific order quantity unless officially confirmed.

4.5 Buyers Verifying Baghlaf Steel
Description

Visitors whose first objective is not necessarily to buy steel immediately, but to determine whether a person, website, email address, or communication channel genuinely belongs to Baghlaf Steel.

This audience is strategically important because digital identity and impersonation concerns are part of the project's core business problem.

Primary Needs

They may want to verify:

Official website
Official email domain
Official phone numbers
Official WhatsApp
Office information
Authorized representatives
Official social profiles
Website Priority

Critical

The Verification experience should be accessible without requiring a visitor to first become a qualified sales lead.

5. Secondary Audience Considerations

The following groups may interact with the website but should not automatically become primary target segments without management evidence:

Existing business contacts
Potential suppliers
Commercial intermediaries
Logistics stakeholders
Industry professionals
Media or researchers
Job seekers

The website architecture may accommodate these visitors, but they should not displace the primary B2B buyer experience.

6. Buyer Needs Framework

Across the primary buyer groups, the website should support five major needs:

1. UNDERSTAND
Who is Baghlaf and what does it do?

2. EVALUATE
Can Baghlaf potentially address my requirement?

3. VERIFY
Is this the genuine Baghlaf Steel?

4. QUALIFY
Can I provide enough information for Baghlaf to assess my requirement?

5. ENGAGE
How do I begin the commercial conversation?

These needs form the basis of the buyer experience.

7. Buyer Questions

A serious buyer should be able to answer the following questions through the website:

Products

9. Buyer Trust Concerns

International buyers may have concerns before initiating a commercial relationship.

Potential concerns include:

Is this a legitimate company?

Am I dealing with the actual Baghlaf Steel?

Who actually supplies the steel?

Is Baghlaf the manufacturer or a trading/supply company?

Are the listed products genuine?

Are the listed contacts official?

Can the company handle my requirement?

What happens after I submit my request?

These are strategic UX concerns.

They should be addressed with factual information and evidence rather than generic trust slogans.

10. Buyer Information Hierarchy

The website should generally prioritize information in this order:

1. Company Identity
2. Relevant Capability
3. Product Information
4. Supply Model
5. Evidence / Verification
6. Commercial Process
7. Requirement Submission

The exact order may change by page depending on user intent.

For example:

Product pages should prioritize product information.
Verification pages should prioritize official identity.
Requirement pages should prioritize form completion.
11. Buyer-Centered Homepage

The homepage should allow a new international visitor to understand the company quickly.

The initial experience should communicate:

Who we are
      ↓
What we do
      ↓
What we can potentially supply
      ↓
How we work
      ↓
How to verify us
      ↓
How to engage

The homepage should not attempt to answer every detailed buyer question.

It should provide strong pathways to the appropriate information.

12. Product Discovery Behavior

A buyer arriving with a specific product need should be able to reach relevant product information quickly.

Ideal path:

Products
   ↓
Category
   ↓
Product
   ↓
Specifications
   ↓
Request This Product

The product experience should avoid unnecessary storytelling before the buyer can reach the relevant technical information.

13. Requirement-First Behavior

Some buyers may already know exactly what they need.

For these users:

Submit Requirement

should be directly accessible from:

Header
Homepage
Product pages
Relevant content sections
Contact page

The website should not force a high-intent buyer through the complete corporate story before allowing them to submit a requirement.

14. Verification-First Behavior

Some visitors may arrive because someone has already contacted them claiming to represent Baghlaf Steel.

For these users:

Verify Baghlaf

should be immediately accessible.

The verification page should not require:

Account creation
Lead submission
Sales contact first
Downloading documents merely to see basic verification information
15. Buyer Friction

The website should minimize unnecessary friction.

Potential friction points include:

Unclear company identity
Hidden contact details
Excessive marketing copy
Difficult product navigation
Unclear terminology
Giant inquiry forms
Mandatory account creation
Poor Arabic/RTL behavior
Slow pages
Unclear error states

The website should remove friction while preserving necessary qualification and security.

20. Technical Buyer vs Commercial Buyer

A single organization may contain multiple stakeholders.

For example:

Commercial Buyer
      ↓
Price / Quantity / Delivery / Terms

Technical Buyer
      ↓
Grade / Standard / Dimensions / Specification

Management
      ↓
Supplier legitimacy / risk / commercial confidence

The website should provide enough information for these stakeholders to evaluate the same company from different perspectives.

21. Multi-Stakeholder Decision Support

The website should support information that can be shared internally.

Examples:

Product page
Technical information
Company overview
Verification page
Official contact page

Each important page should be understandable even when shared independently with another colleague.

22. Shareability

Important pages should have:

Clear page titles
Descriptive URLs
Useful metadata
Clear company identity
Appropriate social/share previews

A buyer should be able to send a product or verification URL to a colleague without requiring additional explanation.

25. Buyer Journey & Supply Sources

Buyers may want to understand where the material comes from.

The website should communicate supply-source information according to the approved disclosure policy.

Exclusivity
Guaranteed availability
Partnership
Authorization

A product page should communicate capability rather than real-time stock unless a verified availability system exists.

Product Capability

28. Arabic Buyer Experience

Arabic is a first-class website experience.

The Arabic experience should provide the same core information and actions as English while respecting native RTL behavior.

Buyer-facing Arabic UX must not be treated as a simple translation layer.

Never invent customer names.

45. Buyer Experience Success Criteria

The website should be considered successful from the buyer perspective when:

✓ A new visitor can understand Baghlaf quickly.

✓ A buyer can find relevant products without unnecessary friction.

✓ A buyer can understand Baghlaf's role in the supply chain.

✓ A skeptical visitor can verify the company independently.

✓ A serious buyer can submit a structured requirement.

✓ Technical and commercial stakeholders can find
  the information they need.

✓ English and Arabic experiences remain usable and professional.

✓ Mobile visitors can complete important tasks.

✓ Buyers receive a clear next step after important actions.

47. Final Strategic Principle

The Baghlaf Steel website should be designed around the real questions, concerns, and actions of international B2B steel buyers. The experience should help buyers understand the company, evaluate its potential supply capability, verify its official identity, and confidently submit a meaningful steel requirement. Broad strategic audience definitions may be established at the project level, but detailed customer segmentation, geographic markets, purchasing behavior, volumes, and buyer profiles must be validated through Baghlaf management and commercial data before being treated as factual.
