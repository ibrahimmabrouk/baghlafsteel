# Baghlaf Steel — Content Strategy & Messaging

> Strategic framework for what Baghlaf Steel communicates, how it communicates, where information appears, and how public content remains accurate, useful, credible, and consistent.

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the content and messaging strategy for the Baghlaf Steel website.

It establishes:

- What the website needs to communicate
- The hierarchy of messages
- The role of content at each stage of the buyer journey
- The voice and tone
- Content principles
- Page-level messaging objectives
- Content governance
- Claim and evidence requirements
- English / Arabic content principles
- AI content-generation rules

This document defines strategic content direction.

It does not replace:

- Management-approved business facts in `docs/02-business/`
- The Claims Matrix
- Final website copy
- Detailed UX specifications
- Approved Figma designs

# 2. Core Content Objective

The website should help an international B2B buyer move from:

```text
Uncertainty
     ↓
Understanding
     ↓
Evaluation
     ↓
Verification
     ↓
Confidence
     ↓
Commercial Engagement

Content should therefore serve a practical purpose:

Help the buyer understand Baghlaf Steel, evaluate its relevance, verify its identity, and know what to do next.

3. Core Content Principle
Clarity Before Persuasion

The website should prioritize:

Clear information
Accurate terminology
Useful technical content
Verifiable facts
Commercial relevance
Easy navigation

over:

Excessive marketing language
Unsupported superlatives
Long corporate statements
Repetitive persuasion
Generic "premium" language

The preferred doctrine remains:

Proof over slogans.

5. Primary Message

The website should establish one clear high-level understanding:

Baghlaf Steel is a Saudi steel trading and supply company serving international B2B buyers.

This is the current strategic message.

The exact public wording should be finalized from management-approved company language.

6. Secondary Message

The website should communicate that Baghlaf helps buyers:

Source suitable steel according to their requirements through appropriate supply sources and commercial coordination.

This is a strategic positioning concept.

It must be refined against the actual commercial process confirmed by management.

Avoid phrases that communicate little information.

Industry-leading excellence
Best-in-class services
Unparalleled quality
Unmatched expertise

34. Avoid Unsupported Superlatives

Do not use:

Largest
Leading
Number one
Best
Most trusted
Fastest
Biggest

unless substantiated and approved.

35. Avoid Manufacturer Language

Do not write:

Our factories
Our mills
Our production
Our manufacturing facilities
Our steel production
Our production capacity

unless the company's actual business model changes and management explicitly approves these statements.

36. Avoid False Scarcity or Urgency

Do not use:

Limited stock
Act now
Last allocation
Immediate availability
Guaranteed delivery

unless those statements are factually true and approved for the specific context.

37. Avoid Manipulative Trust Language

Do not rely on:

"You can trust us."

Prefer:

"Verify our official website, email addresses, and contact channels."

Trust should be created through verifiable information.

39. Claim Types

Content should distinguish:

Fact

Verified and approved information.

Strategic Statement

The intended positioning or messaging direction.

Capability Statement

A statement about what Baghlaf can potentially do, subject to appropriate validation.

Availability Statement

A statement about confirmed current availability.

Testimonial

A statement attributed to a real person/customer with appropriate authorization.

These should never be conflated.

40. Capability vs Guarantee

The content system must distinguish:

Can Potentially Supply
        ≠
Currently Available
        ≠
Guaranteed to Supply

The strongest wording should only be used when the underlying business evidence supports it.

41. Product Availability Language

Unless a verified real-time availability system exists, product pages should prefer language describing:

Product capability / sourcing capability

rather than:

Current stock / guaranteed availability.

42. Quantity Language

Quantities must be treated as factual commercial information.

For example:

100,000 MT

must not be described as:

Minimum order
Standard order
Typical volume
Capacity

unless management confirms the meaning.

43. Supply-Source Language

When a supply source is publicly named, the content should identify the relationship accurately.

Avoid:

Our manufacturing partner

unless the relationship is verified and the phrase is approved.

Prefer the precise approved relationship type.

44. Credential Language

73. Content Failure Modes
Failure 1 — Marketing Without Information

Large claims but little useful detail.

Mitigation

Prioritize concrete information and evidence.

Failure 2 — Manufacturer Confusion

Content accidentally implies Baghlaf manufactures the steel.

Mitigation

Controlled terminology and business-model rules.

Failure 3 — Unsupported Claims

Statements exceed available evidence.

Mitigation

Claims Matrix + approval workflow.

Failure 4 — AI-Generated Generic Copy

Content sounds like an interchangeable AI template.

Mitigation

Use Baghlaf-specific facts, terminology, and buyer questions.

Failure 5 — Excessive Length

Pages become corporate essays.

Mitigation

Structure information for scanning.

Failure 6 — Translation Drift

Arabic changes the factual or commercial meaning.

Mitigation

Meaning-preserving localization + review.

Failure 7 — Inconsistent Facts

Different pages use different company details.

Mitigation

Single structured business source of truth.

81. Final Strategic Principle

Baghlaf Steel's website content should make the company easier to understand, evaluate, verify, and engage with. Content should lead with clarity, factual accuracy, technical usefulness, and evidence rather than generic corporate persuasion.
