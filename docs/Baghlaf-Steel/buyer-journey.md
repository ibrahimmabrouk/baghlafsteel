# Part 6 — Buyer Journey & Conversion Strategy

**Version:** 0.1  
**Scope:** Buyer Journey, Conversion Goals, Information Hierarchy, Calls to Action, Inquiry Flow, and Conversion UX  
**Status:** Working Strategy Draft  
**Primary Domain Direction:** `baghlaf.com.sa`

---

# 6.1 Strategic Objective

The Baghlaf Steel website should not function primarily as an online brochure.

Its primary commercial role is to **move qualified international buyers from uncertainty to confidence, and from confidence to a meaningful commercial inquiry.**

The website should therefore guide the buyer through four psychological stages:

```text
DISCOVER
   ↓
UNDERSTAND
   ↓
TRUST
   ↓
ENGAGE
```

Or, more specifically:

```text
Who are you?
      ↓
What do you do?
      ↓
Can you supply what I need?
      ↓
Can I verify you?
      ↓
How does working with you operate?
      ↓
Can I submit my requirement?
```

The website should make this progression feel natural rather than forcing the visitor through unnecessary steps.

---

# 6.2 Primary Conversion Objective

The primary business conversion should be:

> **A qualified steel requirement submitted to Baghlaf Steel through an official channel.**

This is more valuable than a generic:

> "Contact us"

because it gives the commercial team useful information and communicates that Baghlaf understands industrial procurement.

### Primary CTA

**Submit Steel Requirement**

Alternative wording may be tested later:

- Submit Your Requirement
    
- Request Steel Supply
    
- Discuss Your Requirement
    
- Request a Quote
    

The final CTA should be selected according to management's actual commercial process.

---

# 6.3 Secondary Conversion Objectives

Not every visitor will be ready to submit a requirement.

Therefore the website should support several levels of engagement.

### Level 1 — Trust

**Verify Baghlaf Steel**

For visitors who are primarily validating authenticity.

### Level 2 — Research

**Explore Products**

For visitors evaluating supply capability.

### Level 3 — Understanding

**How We Work**

For visitors evaluating the company's operating model.

### Level 4 — Commercial Interest

**Discuss Your Requirement**

For visitors who are interested but are not ready to complete the full inquiry process.

### Level 5 — Qualified Conversion

**Submit Steel Requirement**

For buyers with a defined commercial need.

# 6.7 First Trust Checkpoint

A significant percentage of unfamiliar visitors may immediately investigate whether the company is legitimate.

This is especially important given the market misinformation described by management.

The website should therefore make **Verification** accessible without requiring the visitor to search for it.

Possible locations:

```text
Header
Hero
Homepage trust section
Footer
Contact page
```

The visitor should never have to ask:

> "Where can I verify this company?"

---

# 6.8 Understanding Stage

Once the visitor accepts that Baghlaf is legitimate, they need to understand what it actually does.

The website should communicate clearly that:

> **Baghlaf Steel is a trading and supply company, not a steel manufacturer.**

This should not be presented apologetically.

Instead, the website should explain the business model positively:

```text
Buyer Requirement
        ↓
Baghlaf Assessment
        ↓
Saudi Supply Sourcing
        ↓
Commercial Coordination
        ↓
Supply & Documentation
```

This makes Baghlaf's role understandable.

---

# 6.9 Capability Evaluation

After understanding the business model, the buyer asks:

> "Can Baghlaf actually satisfy my requirement?"

This is where the website needs evidence.

Potential evidence areas include:

- Product categories
    
- Steel grades
    
- Standards
    
- Available specifications
    
- Supply sources
    
- Geographic reach
    
- Order sizes where appropriate
    
- Supply process
    
- Documentation
    
- Case studies
    
- Credentials
    

Only verified information should be published.

# 6.11 Supply Network Journey

Because Baghlaf's business model depends on sourcing rather than manufacturing, the supply network should help explain:

> **Where does the material come from?**

Potential structure:

```text
Saudi Arabia
      ↓
Qualified / Established Supply Sources
      ↓
Baghlaf Requirement Assessment
      ↓
Buyer-Specific Sourcing
      ↓
International Supply
```

The website must avoid implying ownership of manufacturers or facilities.

The names and logos of specific manufacturers should only be displayed after management verifies the appropriate relationship and permissions.

---

# 6.12 "How We Work" as a Conversion Tool

The process page should reduce uncertainty.

A buyer should understand the steps before submitting an inquiry.

Potential sequence:

### Step 01 — Submit Requirement

Tell Baghlaf what you need.

### Step 02 — Requirement Review

The commercial team reviews the product, quantity, specifications, destination, and requirements.

### Step 03 — Sourcing

Baghlaf identifies an appropriate supply route within Saudi Arabia.

### Step 04 — Commercial Coordination

Commercial and technical details are coordinated.

### Step 05 — Supply

The transaction proceeds according to agreed terms and documentation.

### Step 06 — Follow-Up

Communication and required documentation continue through the agreed process.

The exact commercial sequence should later be validated by management.

```text
      ↓
```

# 6.18 High-Intent vs Low-Intent Visitors

We should design for different buyer readiness levels.

### Low Intent

> "I am researching Saudi steel suppliers."

Give them:

- About
    
- Products
    
- Supply Network
    
- How We Work
    
- Verification
    
- Resources
    

### Medium Intent

> "I may need steel soon."

Give them:

- Product information
    
- Specifications
    
- Supply process
    
- Inquiry CTA
    
- Contact
    

### High Intent

> "I need 100,000 MT of [product] for [destination]."

Give them:

- Direct requirement submission
    
- Fast contact
    
- Technical information
    
- Commercial contact
    
- Official verification
    

The website should support all three without treating them identically.

---

# 6.19 Trust Before Conversion

For a high-value B2B business, forcing an inquiry too early can be counterproductive.

The ideal journey is:

```text
Trust
  ↓
Understanding
  ↓
Capability
  ↓
Confidence
  ↓
Conversion
```

Not:

```text
Homepage
  ↓
BUY NOW
```

The website should feel like an informed commercial conversation, not an e-commerce store.

---

# 6.20 Inquiry Confirmation

After submission, the confirmation page should reinforce trust.

Instead of:

> "Thanks! We'll contact you."

Use something more informative.

For example:

### Requirement Received

> **Your steel requirement has been successfully submitted through the official Baghlaf Steel website.**

Then provide:

- Reference number
    
- Submission date
    
- Official contact information
    
- Expected next step
    
- Official email domain
    
- Contact channel for urgent questions
    

The exact response time should only be advertised if management commits to a real SLA.

Do not promise:

> "We will respond within 1 hour"

unless the company actually operates that way.

---

# 6.21 Verification During Conversion

The inquiry journey should maintain access to verification.

A subtle message could appear near submission:

> **Submitting a requirement through `baghlaf.com.sa` connects you directly with the official Baghlaf Steel website.**

And after submission:

> **For your security, future correspondence should be verified against the official contact information published on this website.**

This reinforces the trust system without distracting from the conversion.

# 6.22 Mobile Conversion

Many B2B visitors will still discover the company through mobile devices.

The mobile experience must therefore prioritize:

```text

WhatsApp
```

The design should remain professional and restrained.

# 6.23 WhatsApp Strategy

WhatsApp may be a valuable commercial channel in the Saudi market, but it should be treated carefully.

The website should display only **official company WhatsApp numbers**.

The ideal relationship is:

```text
Website
   ↓
Official WhatsApp
   ↓
Official Baghlaf Contact
```

The visitor should understand that WhatsApp is an official communication channel rather than an unrelated broker's account.

# 6.28 Content Depth Should Match Buyer Intent

Not every page should be long.

A product page might require technical depth.

A verification page needs clarity.

A homepage needs fast understanding.

A case study needs evidence.

A contact page needs usability.

The website should therefore avoid the common AI-generated pattern where **every section becomes a paragraph of marketing copy**.

---

# 6.29 Conversion Friction Principles

We should reduce unnecessary friction at every stage.

### Avoid

- Generic contact forms
    
- Excessive mandatory fields
    
- Unclear CTAs
    
- Long blocks of promotional text
    
- Requiring account creation
    
- Multiple redirects before inquiry
    
- Unnecessary popups
    
- Aggressive animations
    
- Hidden contact details
    

### Prefer

- Clear actions
    
- Short explanations
    
- Useful technical information
    
- Transparent process
    
- Visible official contacts
    
- Easy document upload
    
- Mobile-friendly forms
    
- Confirmation and reference numbers
    

---

# 6.30 Conversion Without Aggressive Marketing

The design language should remain B2B.

Avoid:

> "Don't miss out!"

> "Act now!"

> "Limited opportunity!"

> "Get the best deal!"

Instead:

> **Submit your steel requirement.**

> **Discuss your specification with our team.**

> **Verify the official Baghlaf Steel contact channels.**

The tone should communicate competence rather than sales pressure.

# 6.33 Returning Buyer Experience

The website should remain useful after the initial trust stage.

A returning buyer may visit specifically to:

- Check a product
    
- Review a specification
    
- Find a contact
    
- Submit another requirement
    
- Verify a contact person
    
- Access a document
    
- Find company information
    

This means the site should have strong utility—not merely a persuasive homepage.

# 6.34 Internationalization

Because the primary audience is international, the website should be designed for at least:

**English + Arabic**

The English experience should be treated as a first-class experience rather than a translated version of Arabic.

Potential future languages should only be added if commercial demand justifies them.

The URL architecture and technical implementation should be planned for localization from the beginning.

# 6.35 Conversion Strategy for the Domain Problem

The choice of `baghlaf.com.sa` should be explicitly integrated into the buyer journey.

At relevant stages, buyers should encounter the same identity:

```text
BAGHLAF STEEL
Saudi Steel Trading & Supply

Official Website
baghlaf.com.sa

Official Email
@baghlaf.com.sa
```

This repetition builds recognition.

Eventually, when a buyer receives an email from:

> `sales@baghlaf.com.sa`

they should already associate that domain with the official Baghlaf Steel website.

# Part 6 — Final Strategic Statement

> **The Baghlaf Steel website should function as a trust-first B2B conversion platform. Its primary purpose is to guide international buyers from discovery and verification through understanding Baghlaf's supply model and capability, ultimately leading qualified buyers to submit a structured steel requirement through the official Baghlaf Steel digital channel. The experience should prioritize clarity, evidence, professional communication, and low-friction conversion rather than aggressive sales tactics or generic contact forms.**
