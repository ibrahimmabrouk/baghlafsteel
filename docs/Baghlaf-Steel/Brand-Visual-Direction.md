# Baghlaf Steel — Business & Website Strategy

**Part 10 — Brand & Visual Direction**

**Version:** 0.1  
**Scope:** Brand personality, visual territory, art direction, color strategy, typography, layout language, imagery, motion, UI character, and anti-template rules  
**Status:** Working Strategy Draft  
**Primary Domain:** `baghlaf.com.sa`

---

# 10.1 Strategic Objective

The visual identity of Baghlaf Steel should accomplish something more difficult than simply looking "professional."

It should make an international buyer immediately perceive:

> **This is a serious Saudi B2B steel trading and supply company.**

The design must communicate:

```text
Industrial credibility
        +
Commercial maturity
        +
Saudi identity
        +
International confidence
        +
Technical precision
        +
Digital trust
```

At the same time, it must avoid visually communicating something that Baghlaf is not:

> **A steel manufacturing plant.**

The website should therefore represent **steel trade, supply, logistics, infrastructure, and commercial coordination**, rather than manufacturing ownership.

---

# 10.2 Visual North Star

The visual north star for Baghlaf Steel is:

# **Saudi Industrial Trade — Refined for the Global Buyer**

The intended impression is:

> **Strong without being aggressive.  
> Premium without being luxurious.  
> Technical without being cold.  
> Saudi without relying on clichés.  
> Modern without looking like a technology startup.**

---

# 10.3 Recommended Visual Direction

After the strategic work completed so far, the strongest direction is:

# **Industrial Editorial**

This combines:

- Industrial visual language
    
- Editorial typography
    
- Strong photography
    
- Structured grids
    
- Technical information
    
- Generous whitespace
    
- Restrained motion
    
- Precise UI
    

It should feel closer to a **high-end industrial trading company** than to a SaaS startup.

---

# 10.4 Alternative Visual Directions Considered

Before locking the visual direction, we should understand the alternatives.

## Direction A — Heavy Industrial

Characteristics:

- Dark backgrounds
    
- Steel textures
    
- Large machinery imagery
    
- Strong condensed typography
    
- High contrast
    
- Dramatic lighting
    

### Strengths

Very clearly industrial.

### Weaknesses

Could accidentally make Baghlaf look like a manufacturer.

Could feel dated or overly aggressive.

### Verdict

**Not recommended as the primary direction.**

---

## Direction B — Corporate Financial

Characteristics:

- Conservative colors
    
- Lots of white
    
- Formal typography
    
- Very restrained visuals
    
- Heavy emphasis on company information
    

### Strengths

Strong credibility.

### Weaknesses

Could feel too generic and financial.

Doesn't communicate the physical nature of steel and supply strongly enough.

### Verdict

Useful as an influence, but not sufficient alone.

---

## Direction C — Technology / AI

Characteristics:

- Gradients
    
- Glassmorphism
    
- Floating cards
    
- Glows
    
- Abstract 3D shapes
    
- Oversized typography
    
- Excessive animations
    

### Strengths

Modern.

### Weaknesses

Completely misaligned with Baghlaf's industry.

Can look AI-generated.

### Verdict

**Reject.**

---

## Direction D — Industrial Editorial

Characteristics:

- Strong editorial typography
    
- Real industrial photography
    
- Structured grids
    
- Technical data
    
- Large visual moments
    
- Controlled color
    
- High whitespace
    
- Subtle motion
    

### Strengths

- Premium
    
- Modern
    
- Industrial
    
- International
    
- Distinctive
    
- Flexible
    
- Less likely to look AI-generated
    

### Verdict

# **Recommended**

# 10.5 Brand Personality

The visual identity should communicate six primary personality traits.

## 1. Credible

The design should feel established and serious.

## 2. Precise

Spacing, typography, data, and alignment should feel intentional.

## 3. Strong

The identity should have visual weight without relying on aggressive aesthetics.

## 4. International

It should comfortably sit beside the websites of major global industrial businesses.

## 5. Saudi

Saudi identity should be visible but sophisticated.

## 6. Human

The company is ultimately a commercial organization dealing with people, not an anonymous industrial machine.

---

# 10.9 Color Philosophy

The palette should derive from the company's environment:

- Steel
    
- Architecture
    
- Saudi landscape
    
- Concrete
    
- Logistics
    
- Industrial materials
    

The palette should not be generated from generic "corporate blue + orange."

The recommendation is to begin with a **neutral industrial base**, then introduce one controlled brand accent.

---

# 10.10 Recommended Color System

The exact colors should be selected during the Figma Design System stage.

For now, establish the hierarchy rather than hard-code hexadecimal values.

```text
Primary Dark
↓
Charcoal / graphite / deep neutral

Primary Light
↓
Warm industrial white

Secondary Neutral
↓
Concrete / cool gray

Brand Accent
↓
Saudi-inspired or steel-inspired accent

Utility Colors
↓
Success / Warning / Error / Information
```

The palette should remain restrained.

---

# 10.11 Color Ratio

A conceptual ratio:

```text
~65–75%
Neutral / Light

~20–30%
Dark / Structural

~5–10%
Brand Accent
```

The accent should **guide attention**, not dominate the interface.

Saudi identity should be communicated through:

- Brand color choices
    
- Photography
    
- Architectural references
    
- Geographic context
    
- Arabic typography
    
- Cultural restraint
    
- Saudi industrial imagery
    

It should not rely on:

- Decorative national symbols
    
- Generic desert photos
    
- Forced "Vision 2030" references
    

The identity should feel authentically Saudi because the business **is Saudi**, not because the design repeatedly announces it.

# 10.13 Typography Philosophy

Typography is one of the most important elements of the brand.

The site needs to work in:

**English + Arabic**

without either language appearing secondary.

The typography should communicate:

- Precision
    
- Authority
    
- Modernity
    
- Readability
    

---

# 10.14 English Typography Direction

The preferred English typography direction is:

> **Contemporary grotesk / neo-grotesk / modern sans-serif**

Characteristics:

- Clean letterforms
    
- Strong numerals
    
- Excellent readability
    
- Multiple weights
    
- Good large-heading performance
    

Avoid overly geometric fonts that make the site look like a startup landing page.

---

# 10.15 Arabic Typography Direction

The Arabic typeface should:

- Work harmoniously with the English typeface
    
- Remain highly readable
    
- Support large headlines
    
- Maintain professional corporate character
    
- Work well across desktop and mobile
    

Arabic typography should be chosen as a **design decision**, not simply as a fallback font.

---

# 10.16 Bilingual Typography Relationship

English and Arabic should share a common visual hierarchy.

Conceptually:

```text
English H1
        ↕
Arabic H1

English H2
        ↕
Arabic H2

English Body
        ↕
Arabic Body
```

The visual density should feel balanced between languages.

Arabic may naturally require different line lengths and spacing.

The design system must accommodate that rather than forcing English measurements onto Arabic.

---

# 10.17 Type Scale

The final scale will be determined in Figma, but the system should establish clear hierarchy:

```text
Display
H1
H2
H3
H4
Body Large
Body
Body Small
Caption
Label
```

Avoid using giant headings simply to create visual impact.

Typography should serve hierarchy.

---

# 10.18 Numerals and Technical Data

Steel trading requires numbers.

Therefore numerals are especially important.

The type system should handle:

- Metric tons
    
- Dimensions
    
- Grades
    
- Standards
    
- Dates
    
- Quantities
    
- Reference numbers
    
- Phone numbers
    
- Product codes
    

Clean numerical presentation should become part of the visual language.

---

# 10.19 Layout Philosophy

The layout should feel **structural**.

Think:

```text
Grid
Columns
Alignment
Hierarchy
Rhythm
Whitespace
```

rather than:

```text
Floating objects
Random cards
Decorative blobs
```

The site's layout should subtly evoke engineering and industrial structure.

---

# 10.20 Grid System

The design system should be based on a consistent grid.

Conceptually:

```text
Desktop
12-column grid

Tablet
8-column grid

Mobile
4-column grid
```

The exact measurements will be established in Figma.

The important principle:

> **Every major element should have a relationship to the grid.**

---

# 10.21 Section Rhythm

Pages should alternate between:

```text
Information
      ↓
Visual
      ↓
Information
      ↓
Proof
      ↓
Action
```

This creates rhythm.

Avoid repeating:

```text
Heading
Paragraph
Three Cards
Heading
Paragraph
Three Cards
Heading
Paragraph
Three Cards
```

That pattern is one of the clearest signs of AI-generated website structure.

---

# 10.22 Card Philosophy

Cards should be used when they provide a real structural benefit.

Use cards for:

- Products
    
- Contacts
    
- Credentials
    
- Documents
    
- Supply sources
    
- Case studies
    

Avoid putting every paragraph inside a rounded rectangle.

---

# 10.23 Card Shape

The card system should be restrained.

Recommended direction:

- Moderate corner radius
    
- Strong spacing
    
- Thin borders where useful
    
- Minimal shadows
    
- Strong typography
    
- Clear hierarchy
    

Avoid:

> Every section = floating rounded card.

---

# 10.24 Borders and Dividers

Thin structural lines can reinforce the industrial/editorial language.

Potential uses:

```text
Section dividers
Table borders
Navigation separators
Metadata rows
Technical information
Footer structure
```

The line treatment should remain subtle.

# 10.27 Imagery to Avoid

Do not use generic imagery in a way that implies:

> **"This is Baghlaf's facility."**

Avoid presenting:

- Unrelated steel mills
    
- Generic factories
    
- Random furnaces
    
- Foreign manufacturing plants
    
- Stock executives as Baghlaf management
    
- Unrelated warehouses
    

unless clearly contextualized as illustrative imagery.

---

# 10.28 Photography Art Direction

The photography should generally favor:

- Wide compositions
    
- Strong geometry
    
- Natural textures
    
- Controlled contrast
    
- Real environments
    
- Authentic people
    
- Documentary feeling
    

Avoid excessive stock-photo polish.

Realistic imagery contributes more to credibility.

---

# 10.29 Image Treatment

Images may use subtle treatment:

- Cropping
    
- Aspect-ratio consistency
    
- Controlled overlays
    
- Editorial framing
    
- Monochrome treatment where appropriate
    

Avoid heavy color filters that make every image look artificial.

---

# 10.30 Hero Image Strategy

The hero should not necessarily show a giant steel factory.

Better concepts:

### Concept A — Material

Close-up steel texture/product.

### Concept B — Scale

Steel cargo / industrial logistics.

### Concept C — Geography

Saudi industrial environment with strong architectural composition.

### Concept D — Trade

Steel movement through port/logistics infrastructure.

The final hero should be selected based on the message hierarchy.

---

# 10.31 Product Imagery

Product pages should prioritize **accuracy over cinematic beauty**.

The image should allow the buyer to understand:

- What the product looks like
    
- Its form
    
- Its scale
    
- Its packaging/handling where relevant
    

Technical diagrams may be more useful than photographs for some products.

---

# 10.32 Data Visualization

Data visualizations should be simple and purposeful.

Possible uses:

```text
Supply routes
Product specifications
Order process
Geographic reach
Technical comparisons
```

Avoid decorative charts with meaningless numbers.

---

# 10.33 Map Strategy

Maps may be used to communicate Saudi supply geography or international reach.

However:

> **Never create fictional supply locations.**

Every displayed geographic relationship needs verified source information.

A map should communicate information, not decoration.

---

# 10.34 Iconography

Use a consistent icon family.

Recommended characteristics:

- Simple
    
- Technical
    
- Minimal
    
- Linear or restrained
    
- Consistent stroke width
    

Avoid mixing unrelated icon styles.

---

# 10.35 Icon Usage

Icons should clarify information.

Examples:

- Specification
    
- Quantity
    
- Location
    
- Documentation
    
- Contact
    
- Verification
    
- Process
    

Do not add icons merely to make sections appear more designed.

---

# 10.36 Motion Philosophy

Motion should communicate:

**Precision + Flow + Materiality**

rather than:

**Entertainment.**

The website should feel alive without constantly moving.

---

# 10.37 Recommended Motion

Subtle interactions such as:

- Navigation transitions
    
- Image reveals
    
- Section entrance
    
- Number transitions
    
- Hover states
    
- Accordion expansion
    
- Smooth page transitions
    

Motion should generally be quick and restrained.

---

# 10.38 Motion to Avoid

Avoid:

- Constant floating animations
    
- Excessive parallax
    
- Giant text animations
    
- Cursor-following effects
    
- Random 3D effects
    
- Infinite loops everywhere
    
- Excessive page transitions
    

These can reduce perceived professionalism.

---

# 10.44 Button Philosophy

Buttons should be:

- Strong
    
- Compact
    
- Consistent

# 10.45 Forms

Forms should feel professional and simple.

Use:

- Clear labels
    
- Helpful descriptions
    
- Logical sections
    
- Strong validation
    
- Error messages
    
- Upload indicators
    
- Confirmation states
    

Avoid:

- Decorative form fields
    
- Tiny labels
    
- Placeholder-only labels
    
- Unclear required fields

- Clean
    
- Structured
    
- Responsive

- Horizontal scrolling
    
- Stacked presentation
    
- Priority fields
    

The choice should depend on the data.

The site should not simply "shrink desktop."

It should have deliberate responsive compositions.

### Desktop

### Tablet

### Mobile

Clear priority:

```text
Identity
↓
Trust
↓
Products
↓
Process
↓
CTA
```

Mobile should preserve hierarchy, not every desktop component.

# 10.57 Anti-Template Principle

The website should have a visual identity that could be recognized even without the logo.

That means:

- Distinctive typography
    
- Consistent grid
    
- Unique image treatment
    
- Strong structural rhythm
    
- Controlled use of color
    
- Consistent technical presentation
    
- Purposeful motion
    

The design should not feel interchangeable with another company's website.

---

# 10.58 AI Design Rules

The visual direction should eventually become part of the AI design skill.

```text
AI DESIGN RULES

1. Do not use generic SaaS aesthetics.

2. Do not default to purple/blue gradients.

3. Do not use glassmorphism as a default pattern.

4. Do not create decorative cards without
   a semantic purpose.

5. Do not use oversized typography simply
   to make a page look impressive.

6. Do not imply Baghlaf owns a manufacturing
   facility through imagery.

7. Use real industrial and trade context.

8. Favor grids, structure, hierarchy,
   and whitespace.

9. Use motion to communicate meaning.

10. Every visual element must serve a purpose.

11. Maintain a premium B2B industrial character.

12. Maintain equal design quality in Arabic
    and English.

13. Follow the approved Figma system rather than
    inventing new styles per page.
```

# 10.71 Existing Brand vs New Digital Identity

An important strategic decision remains:

### Scenario A

Baghlaf already has an established visual identity.

→ Website should evolve it.

### Scenario B

Brand identity is weak/outdated.

→ Website can modernize the digital expression while preserving recognizable assets.

### Scenario C

No meaningful identity system exists.

→ Build a focused digital-first visual system.

Management should clarify which scenario applies.

---

# 10.72 Logo Strategy

The website should not redesign the company's logo merely to make the website look modern.

The correct approach is:

> **Preserve recognizable corporate identity; modernize the surrounding visual system.**

Any logo redesign should be treated as a separate branding project requiring management approval.

# 10.80 Part 10 — Strategic Design Rules

The design system should follow these principles:

```text
1. Look like a serious industrial business.

2. Never look like a generic AI website.

3. Never imply Baghlaf manufactures steel.

4. Use real-world trade and supply imagery.

5. Prefer evidence and information over decoration.

6. Make Saudi identity authentic and restrained.

7. Build equal-quality English and Arabic experiences.

8. Use strong typography and structured grids.

9. Use cards only when semantically useful.

10. Use motion sparingly and purposefully.

11. Maintain consistent visual hierarchy.

12. Make official identity and verification easy to find.

13. Avoid unsupported visual representations.

14. Let content determine composition.

15. Treat the visual system as a reusable design language,
    not a collection of one-off page designs.
```
