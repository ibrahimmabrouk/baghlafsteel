# English Content

## Purpose

This document is the controlled source for Baghlaf Steel's English-language website content.

It contains approved or reviewable English wording for:

- company pages;
- homepage sections;
- product pages;
- supply-network content;
- trust and verification content;
- buyer journey content;
- forms;
- CTAs;
- resources;
- navigation;
- system messages;
- SEO-facing content where appropriate.

This document is responsible for the **actual English expression** of approved information.

It does not replace:

- business facts;
- product data;
- supply-source records;
- credentials;
- claims;
- terminology;
- messaging strategy;
- SEO strategy.

---

2. Content Status

Each content item should have a status.

Status	Meaning
Planned	Content is required but has not been drafted.
Draft	Initial English wording exists.
Under Review	Wording is currently being reviewed.
Pending Business Verification	Factual information still requires verification.
Pending Business Approval	Content is verified but not approved for public use.
Approved	Content is approved for implementation/publication.
Implemented	Approved content has been implemented.
Published	Content is live in production.
Needs Update	Published content no longer fully reflects approved information.
Deprecated	Content should no longer be used.
Archived	Historical content retained for reference.
Blocked	Required information or decision is missing.

4. Writing Principles

English website content should be:

clear;
precise;
professional;
confident;
direct;
calm;
commercially appropriate;
internationally understandable.

The preferred tone is:

Professional without being corporate-heavy. Confident without exaggeration. Commercial without sounding aggressive.

33. Claims Control

Before publishing an English sentence that contains a factual or evaluative claim:

1. Identify the claim.
2. Check claims-matrix.md.
3. Verify the underlying fact.
4. Confirm conditions.
5. Preserve approved wording.

A rewrite must not accidentally strengthen a claim.

36. Writing for International Buyers

English should be understandable to buyers from different countries and English-language backgrounds.

Prefer:

plain professional English;
specific terminology;
short useful sentences;
clear units;
explicit processes.

Avoid:

idioms;
excessive metaphors;
slang;
culturally narrow expressions;
unnecessary buzzwords.
37. Steel Industry Language

Technical language should be used accurately.

Do not simplify a technical term if doing so changes its meaning.

Where a technical term is internationally recognized, preserve the standard technical notation.

Examples:

ASTM
EN
ISO
MT
mm

should remain technically recognizable where applicable.
