# Messaging

## Purpose

This document defines the messaging strategy for the Baghlaf Steel website.

It answers:

- What should Baghlaf communicate?
- What should buyers understand first?
- What should make the company credible?
- What should differentiate Baghlaf?
- What should each major page communicate?
- What actions should the messaging lead buyers toward?

This document defines **message direction and hierarchy**, not the final production copy.

Final public wording must remain consistent with:

2. Messaging Status

Messaging may exist in different states:

Status	Meaning
Strategic	Approved as a direction, but not necessarily final copy.
Draft	Proposed messaging not yet approved.
Under Review	Being reviewed by the relevant stakeholders.
Business Pending	Depends on unverified business information.
Approved	Approved for use as a messaging direction.
Implemented	Applied to website content.
Deprecated	No longer part of the intended messaging system.
3. Primary Communication Objective

The website should help an international B2B buyer quickly understand:

Who is Baghlaf?
        ↓
What does Baghlaf do?
        ↓
What can Baghlaf supply / source?
        ↓
How does the process work?
        ↓
How can I verify the company?
        ↓
How do I submit my requirement?

The site should reduce uncertainty before asking the buyer to take commercial action.

4. Core Messaging Principle

The central communication principle is:

Clarity before persuasion.

The website should first establish understanding and trust, then encourage commercial action.

The preferred sequence is:

Clarity
→
Relevance
→
Evidence
→
Trust
→
Action
5. Core Message

The current strategic core message is:

Baghlaf Steel is a Saudi steel trading and supply company serving international B2B buyers.

Status:

Pending Management Approval

This statement must not become stronger during copywriting without corresponding business approval.

6. Core Commercial Idea

The central commercial idea is:

From Requirement to Supply

This expresses the intended role of Baghlaf as a company that helps move a buyer's steel requirement toward an appropriate supply solution.

It does not automatically mean:

Baghlaf manufactures the steel;
Baghlaf owns manufacturing facilities;
Baghlaf holds inventory;
every product is immediately available;
every requirement is guaranteed.

The exact public wording remains subject to business approval.

7. Messaging Hierarchy

The website should generally communicate in this order:

1. Identity
2. Business Role
3. Product / Supply Capability
4. Process
5. Evidence
6. Verification
7. Commercial Action

This hierarchy should guide both page structure and copywriting.

8. Primary Audience

The primary audience is:

International B2B Steel Buyers

Messaging should be appropriate for buyers such as:

procurement professionals;
steel buyers;
industrial procurement teams;
commodity buyers;
sourcing teams;
companies evaluating steel supply options.

The content should prioritize commercial clarity over generic corporate storytelling.

9. Buyer Questions

Messaging should answer the buyer's most important questions.

Identity
Who is Baghlaf?
Where is the company based?
What does the company actually do?
Capability
What products can Baghlaf supply/source?
What forms/specifications are available?
Supply

What is Baghlaf's relationship with those sources?
Process

Trust

How can I verify the company?
Which contact channels are official?
Action
How do I submit my requirement?
How do I contact the company?
10. Message Pillars

The current strategic messaging system contains six primary pillars.

Pillar 1 — Saudi Supply Access

Communicate Baghlaf's Saudi-based position and access to relevant steel supply, subject to approved business facts.

The message should emphasize:

Saudi Context
+
Supply Access
+
Commercial Relevance

Avoid implying:

Ownership
Manufacturing
Exclusivity

unless explicitly approved.

Pillar 2 — Requirement-Based Sourcing

Emphasize that the buyer can start with an actual steel requirement rather than navigating an abstract catalog alone.

Conceptually:

Buyer Requirement
→
Assessment
→
Sourcing
→
Commercial Coordination

The actual process must follow the approved business workflow.

Pillar 3 — Commercial Coordination

Communicate Baghlaf's role in helping move the requirement through the commercial process.

Do not claim responsibilities that management has not confirmed.

For example, the website should not imply that Baghlaf independently controls:

production;
inspection;
shipping;
customs;
financing;
logistics;

unless those activities are actually part of the company's role.

Pillar 4 — Transparency

Messaging should reduce ambiguity about:

company role;
source relationships;
products;
process;
verification;
contact channels.

Transparency should be expressed through precise information, not vague promises.

Pillar 5 — Verification & Trust

Trust is a functional part of the messaging system.

The website should make it easy for buyers to:

Identify
→
Verify
→
Contact
→
Engage Safely

The strongest trust message is not:

"We are the most trusted."

It is:

Clear identity + official channels + supporting evidence + transparent process.

Pillar 6 — International B2B Orientation

The tone should communicate that the website is designed for professional international buyers.

Use:

clear commercial language;
international terminology where appropriate;
technical precision;
structured product information;
straightforward processes.

Avoid unnecessary slogans and consumer-style persuasion.

11. Desired Perception

The messaging should encourage buyers to perceive Baghlaf as:

Credible
Capable
Precise
Transparent
Professional
International
Saudi
Commercially Serious

These are desired perceptions, not automatically factual claims.

They should be created through the quality of the information and experience rather than unsupported adjectives.

12. Differentiation

The messaging should differentiate Baghlaf from:

Manufacturers
Generic Steel Traders
Brokers / Intermediaries
Marketplaces
Unverified Online Sellers

The website should do this by explaining Baghlaf's actual role, not by attacking competitors.

Potential differentiation themes:

Clear Business Identity
Requirement-Based Sourcing
Supply Access
Commercial Coordination
Verification
Transparency

Only themes supported by the approved business model should become public claims.

13. Manufacturer Boundary in Messaging

Messaging must clearly preserve the distinction between:

Baghlaf Steel
        ≠
External Manufacturer

Where relevant, the website should explain the role accurately.

Avoid language such as:

Our mills
Our factories
Our production lines
We manufacture
We produce

unless explicitly approved.

Preferred:

Approved Product Information

16. Products Messaging

The Products section should answer:

What can Baghlaf help me source?

It should communicate:

product categories;
relevant forms;
approved technical information;
applications where supported;
next-step action.

The product catalog should not imply current inventory unless availability is separately confirmed.

17. Product Detail Messaging

A product detail page should answer:

What is this product?
What specifications matter?
What forms/grades are relevant?
Can Baghlaf source it?
What should I do next?

The page should move naturally toward:

Submit Requirement

rather than:

Buy Now

unless the actual business model supports direct online purchase.

18. Supply Network Messaging

The Supply Network page should answer:

How does Baghlaf connect buyer requirements with appropriate supply sources?

The message should emphasize:

Supply Relationships
+
Requirement Matching
+
Commercial Coordination

Do not present the page as a public supplier marketplace or complete database of every available manufacturer.

19. How We Work Messaging

The page should reduce uncertainty about the commercial journey.

Conceptual sequence:

Submit Requirement
→
Review
→
Source / Coordinate
→
Commercial Progress
→
Fulfillment

The final sequence requires confirmation of Baghlaf's actual process.

Do not promise timing, outcomes, or guarantees without approval.

22. Verification Messaging

Verification should be treated as an important buyer need, not a footnote.

Core message:

The exact public wording requires approval.

Official Identity

23. Contact Messaging

The Contact page should answer:

How can I reach Baghlaf through an official channel?

It should favor:

clarity;
official channels;
relevant contact paths;
verification.

Avoid presenting every available contact method with equal visual weight if management has established a clear hierarchy.

24. Requirement Messaging

The requirement flow should communicate:

Tell us what you need.

The experience should make it clear that the buyer is submitting a requirement for commercial review, not automatically placing an order.

The messaging should reduce uncertainty about:

what information is needed;
why it is needed;
what happens after submission;
how the buyer receives a reference;
how communication continues.

Do not invent response-time commitments.

Every major page should offer enough trust context for its user intent.

28. Primary vs Secondary Messages

Every major page should have:

One Primary Message
+
Supporting Messages
+

Avoid giving every section equal messaging priority.

29. Message Length

B2B buyers should not need to read large paragraphs to understand core facts.

Prefer:

Clear Headline
+
Short Explanation
+
Structured Evidence
+
Action

Longer explanations should be used where complexity genuinely requires them.

30. Message Density

Information density should match buyer intent.

For example:

Homepage

High clarity, moderate detail.

Product Detail

Higher technical detail.

Verification

High factual density.

Requirement Form

High action clarity, low unnecessary marketing.

33. Anti-Misrepresentation Rules

Messaging must never imply:

Baghlaf owns another company's factory
Baghlaf manufactures a product it does not manufacture
A source relationship is exclusive when it is not
A product is available when availability is unknown
A credential belongs to Baghlaf when it belongs to another entity
A requirement is an order
A prospect is a customer
A strategic direction is an established fact
34. International English

English messaging should be understandable to buyers with different English proficiency levels.

Prefer:

Plain
Professional
Specific
Direct

Avoid:

Idioms
Slang
Overly poetic language
Excessive corporate jargon

Official Channels

38. Messaging and SEO

SEO should work from the approved messaging system.

Search optimization may improve discoverability, but should not change the business meaning.

For example:

Core Message:
Saudi Steel Trading & Supply

should not become:

Saudi Arabia's Leading Global Steel Manufacturer

merely to capture additional keywords.

39. Message Testing

Where practical, important messaging may be tested against buyer questions:

Can the buyer understand what Baghlaf does?
Can the buyer understand whether Baghlaf is a manufacturer?
Can the buyer understand how products are sourced?
Can the buyer verify the company?
Can the buyer understand what happens after submission?
Can the buyer find the next action?

The purpose is clarity, not simply conversion optimization.
