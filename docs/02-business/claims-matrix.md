# Claims Matrix

## Purpose

This document is the authoritative control register for public-facing claims made about Baghlaf Steel.

It defines which claims are:

- approved;
- conditionally approved;
- restricted;
- pending verification;
- pending management approval;
- prohibited.

The purpose is to prevent unsupported, exaggerated, misleading, or ambiguous statements from appearing anywhere in the Baghlaf Steel digital presence.

This includes claims appearing in:

- website copy;
- product pages;
- headings;
- CTAs;
- metadata;
- SEO content;
- structured data;
- downloadable documents;
- forms;
- confirmation messages;
- emails;
- social previews;
- advertisements;
- AI-generated content;
- images containing text;
- future digital products.

---

# 1. Core Principle

A claim must not be published simply because it:

- sounds professional;
- is common in the steel industry;
- appears on another website;
- was suggested by an AI model;
- appears in a search result;
- appears in an old company document;
- is technically plausible.

A public claim requires an appropriate source and approval.

The governing principle is:

> No evidence + no approval = no public claim.

---

# 2. Claim Status

Use the following controlled statuses:

| Status | Meaning | Public Use |
|---|---|---|
| Approved | Verified and explicitly approved for public use. | Yes |
| Conditionally Approved | May be used only under specified wording or conditions. | Yes, within conditions |
| Restricted | May be used internally but not publicly without additional approval. | No |
| Pending Verification | Evidence or factual verification is incomplete. | No |
| Pending Approval | Verified but not yet approved for public communication. | No |
| Prohibited | Must not be used. | No |
| Unknown | No reliable information is currently available. | No |

AI agents and developers must respect these statuses exactly.

38. Source Priority for Claims

When determining whether a claim is supported, prefer:

1. Management-Approved Business Information
2. Approved Corporate / Commercial Documentation
3. Explicitly Approved Supplier / Manufacturer Documentation
4. Approved Project Decision
5. Other Explicitly Accepted Evidence
6. External Research

External research should not silently become Baghlaf's business truth.

39. Conflict Handling

If two sources conflict:

Do not guess.
Do not merge them.
Do not select the more impressive claim.
Do not publish.

Flag Conflict
→ Identify Sources
→ Identify Responsible Authority
→ Resolve
→ Record Approved Position

41. Claim Risk Levels

Claims may also be assigned a risk level.

Critical

Could materially misrepresent the company, legal position, commercial relationship, or customer.

Examples:

manufacturer status;
ownership;
authorization;
exclusivity;
certification;
customer relationship.
High

Could materially affect commercial expectations.

Examples:

availability;
capacity;
delivery;
pricing;
volume;
guarantees.
Medium

Could influence perception but is less likely to create a direct commercial misunderstanding.

Examples:

experience;
reliability;
quality;
market presence.
Low

General descriptive language with limited factual risk.

Even low-risk claims should remain truthful.

42. AI Claim Enforcement

Before generating or modifying public-facing content, AI agents should ask:

1. Is this a factual claim?
2. What is the source?
3. Is the source authoritative?
4. Is the claim approved?
5. Are there conditions?
6. Does the exact wording preserve the approved meaning?

If the answer is unknown:

Do not publish.
43. Claim Diff Review

When an AI rewrites public content, compare:

Previous Approved Meaning
            ↓
New Wording
            ↓
Meaning Preserved?

A stylistic rewrite should not accidentally strengthen a claim.

Examples:

"Can source"
→ "Supplies"

"Supply source"
→ "Authorized supplier"

"International buyers"
→ "Global customers"

"Product capability"
→ "Available stock"

"Example quantity"
→ "Minimum order quantity"

These are not equivalent statements.
